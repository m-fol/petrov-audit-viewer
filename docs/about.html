Audit viewer for satellite fire alerts

A small, local tool for annotators who judge satellite fire alerts by eye. For each alert it shows clear Sentinel-2 images from before and after the alert date, a blink view, 3× close-ups, and records four answers: fire visible, land context, likely cause and confidence.

The example website

The main page of this site is the page two annotators used for the human check: 80 alerts from eastern Ukraine, each with clear Sentinel-2 images from before and after the alert date. The answers have been removed, so you can try it as an annotator. The two context panels here use open data (an ESA WorldCover land-cover map and a wider Sentinel-2 view); the study itself used basemap views from a commercial imagery service, which are not included for licence reasons.

What it is for

Satellite fire detections say that something was hot, not why. The viewer supports a blind check of why: annotators compare images from before and after the alert and choose a cause (agricultural, wildfire, industrial or static, military, or unclear). It was used for the human label check in the paper Not Observed Is Not Calm: Where Open Sensors Go Blind and How to Verify Alerts for C2 (ICMCIS 2027 submission).

Honest limits. Sentinel-2 pixels are 10 m. Small burns, craters and vehicles are usually not visible, so many answers are legitimately “unclear”. In the study the two annotators agreed little (Cohen’s kappa 0.13 for the cause). The tool is an instrument, not evidence that ten-metre images settle the cause of a fire.

How to use it for your own alerts

  1. Download the repository and install the requirements (pip install -r requirements.txt).
  2. Make a CSV like example/alerts_example.csv (columns alert_id, date, lat, lon plus empty answer columns).
  3. Run python build_audit_viewer.py my_alerts.csv my_viewer --exclude-unusable. Images come from the public Microsoft Planetary Computer; no account is needed.
  4. Give the annotator the output folder. They open index.html, answer, and click Export CSV once at the end.

Privacy

This site has no server, no analytics and no account. On the example website, answers are kept only in your own browser (local storage) and are never sent anywhere. Use Export CSV to keep them.

Data and terms

The images contain modified Copernicus Sentinel-2 data (2022 to 2024), free and open under the Copernicus data policy, and the land-cover panels use ESA WorldCover 2021 (CC BY 4.0, © ESA WorldCover consortium). The tool can also fetch basemap views from the Esri World Imagery service; they are not included here, and you should check Esri’s terms before using or sharing them.
